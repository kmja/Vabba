The short answer on fractional days

You asked whether you can request 1,7 days in a single week. You cannot — but your instinct about averaging is exactly right, and it is the reason the app's numbers are still usable.

Föräldrapenning is granted per calendar day at one of five levels — hel, tre fjärdedels, halv, en fjärdedels, en åttondels dag. Nothing finer exists. So a single week's total must land on a multiple of ⅛, and 1,7 is not one of them.

Over a longer stretch it stops mattering. You file for specific dates, so any average that resolves to whole days over a repeating cycle is filable exactly as the plan describes it — 1,6 days a week is 8 whole days every 5 weeks, and you choose which 8. That is what every pace in this app means: an average, not a weekly instruction.

Pace shown Filable as one week? Whole-day cycle What you actually file
0,5Yes — halv dag2 weeks1 day per fortnight
1,6No — nearest 1,5 or 1,6255 weeks8 days per 5 weeks
2,3No — nearest 2,25 or 2,37510 weeks23 days per 10 weeks
3,7No — nearest 3,625 or 3,7510 weeks37 days per 10 weeks
5,0Yes1 week5 days every week

The one place averaging breaks. After the child turns 1, the SGI rule is a test on each week, not on the average. A plan that shows exactly 5,0 has to be five days in every week — you cannot bank three quiet weeks and take twenty days in the fourth. Below the first birthday SGI is protected regardless, so averaging is free there.

None of this was anywhere in the app. "1,6 dagar/vecka" was presented as though you could ask for it, and a user who tried would have been told no.

Fixed in v0.29.0Every period block now spells out the cycle underneath the pace: "Takten är ett snitt: 1,3 dagar/vecka betyder 13 hela dagar var 10:e vecka — du väljer själv vilka dagar. Hos Försäkringskassan tar du ut hela, ¾, halva, ¼ eller ⅛ dagar, så en enskild vecka kan inte bli just den här siffran." A pace that is already a clean weekly number says so without the caveat.

RuleSFB 12:9FK deltid guidance

01Part-time salary is divided by a 7-day week instead of a 5-day one

partTimeSalary = salary × (7 − pace) / 7 treats the working week as seven days. Försäkringskassan sizes partial days against normal working time — five days. The app's own rules.ts comment states the correct mapping (75 % work → 1,25 dagar/vecka, 50 % → 2,5) and the code then contradicts it.

pace app salary% FK-consistent error 1,25 82,1 % 75,0 % +7,1 pp 2,0 71,4 % 60,0 % +11,4 pp 2,5 64,3 % 50,0 % +14,3 pp 3,75 46,4 % 25,0 % +21,4 pp On a 40 000 kr salary at pace 1,25: +2 857 kr/mån too much.

This inflates household net on every part-time plan, and because solveBudget picks the slowest pace that clears a net floor, an inflated net makes it pick a slower pace than the floor really allows — the error compounds into the schedule, not just the display.

Fixed in v0.29.0Both copies of the formula collapsed into one exported partTimeSalaryAt() measured against a 5-day week, so they cannot drift apart again. A part-timer at 2,5 dagar/vecka now keeps 50 % of salary, not 64,3 %. The budget solver reads the corrected figure, so the pace it picks moved with it.

Sourcegoal-seek.ts:563optimizer.ts:329

RuleSFB 12:4 a

02Dubbeldagar are only real if the first caregiver happens to be at pace 7

A dubbeldag is a day both parents draw föräldrapenning. The optimizer correctly charges one day to each parent. The solver then schedules only the second caregiver's side of it, at pace 7, and lets the first caregiver carry on at whatever pace their own goal produced.

20-day window, both caregivers "home": first caregiver at pace 7 B draws 20, A draws 20,00 100 % real first caregiver at pace 0,5 B draws 20, A draws 1,43 7 % real ↑ the app's default pace

In the default configuration the family is debited 40 days for roughly 1,4 genuine dubbeldagar. The first caregiver's other ~18,6 charged days do not vanish — they quietly become ordinary solo days later in their own stretch, so the plan is longer than it should be and the "20 dubbeldagar" headline describes something the schedule never delivers.

Fixed in v0.29.0The window is now settled before anyone is solved and handed to both caregivers: the second gets their concurrent stretch as before, the first gets a full-day stretch pinned into their own pace schedule over the same dates. Measured at three goal modes, including the slow default — 20 of 20 days are now days both of them draw. The window is also bounded by whichever pot is smaller.

RuleSFB 12:12–13TIMING.sjukpenningUntilAge

03The solver has no idea the deadlines exist

solvePlan never looks at planDeadlines. It will happily lay income-based days across years in which they are already forfeit. The warnings that do exist compare day counts against calendar days from today — they never inspect the schedule that was actually produced.

Part-time caregiver, "as long as possible", floor 0 kr: 2026-06-01 .. 2027-06-01 pace 0,5 income 2027-06-01 .. 2033-11-21 pace 0,5 income ← past the 4-year deadline 2033-11-21 .. 2035-08-13 pace 0,5 lägstanivå ends 2035-08-13 — the child is 9,2 years old overruns the income-day deadline by 1 899 days warnings raised: none

TIMING.maxDaysSavedFromAge4 (96) is declared in the ruleset and never enforced anywhere in the codebase.

Fixed in v0.29.0deadlinePaceFloor() gives every solver a pace floor derived from days-remaining against calendar-remaining, rounded up to the tenth the solvers quantise to. The age-9 plan now finishes its income days three months inside the deadline and ends at 5,5 years on lägstanivå days, which legitimately run to 12. Where no pace can fit — more pott than calendar — PlanSolve.incomeDaysPastDeadline reports the loss from the solved schedule and the results page raises it as critical.

RuleKollektivavtalnot statute

06Föräldralön stretches without any calendar limit

months = round(months × 7 / pace) has no ceiling. A six-month employer top-up taken at 0,5 dagar/vecka is reported as running for 84 months. Collective agreements generally require it to be drawn within 18–24 months of the birth.

pace monthly months monthly×months total 7,0 5 601 6 33 606 33 605 5,0 4 001 8 32 008 33 605 −4,8 % 2,5 2 000 17 34 000 33 605 0,5 400 84 33 600 33 605 child is 7

Secondary: the per-month figure and the total disagree by up to ~5 %, so the breakdown does not reconcile with the headline.

Fixed in v0.29.0A window — SUPPLEMENT_WINDOW_MONTHS, 18 by default and overridable per agreement — now caps the stretch, and the total counts only what is actually paid inside it. Months are kept to one decimal instead of rounded to whole months, which is where the 5 % went; total === round(monthly × months) is now asserted. A plan whose pace outruns the window says so.

Sourcenowhere in src/

RuleFLL 5 §, 6 §, 7 §

07Föräldraledighetslagen is absent — the right to be off is not the money

The app models föräldrapenning entitlement and treats it as though it also granted time away from work. It does not. Three separate rights govern that:

FLL 5 § full leave until the child is 18 months, with or without FP FLL 6 § after that, leave only for the fraction you draw FP for FLL 7 § up to 25 % reduced hours until the child turns 8, no FP needed

So the default plan's "0,5 dagar/vecka through the first year" is lawful leave under 5 §. The same pace at 20 months is not — past 18 months a caregiver drawing 0,5 days a week has the right to be off 0,5 days a week, plus a quarter of their hours. The app draws these plans without comment.

Fixed in v0.29.0All three rights are now stated on the results page. Worth noting what the fix could not be: a warning that fires on bad plans. Once the expiry floor and the SGI floor are both in place, a non-part-timer is never scheduled below 5 dagar/vecka after the first year, and a part-timer is working the rest of the week by definition — so the condition has no plans left to flag. It is context, and it is placed as context.

RuleFK 90-day limitFLL 13 §

08The two deadlines that actually bite in practice are missing

A plan is only worth what you can still claim. Föräldrapenning must be applied for within 90 days of the day being claimed, and an employer is owed two months' notice of leave under FLL 13 §. Neither appears anywhere in the app — and the second one shapes when a plan has to be settled, not just filed.

Fixed in v0.29.0Both now appear in "Bra att veta" at the foot of the results page. Finding them a home meant an unrelated repair: results.tsx filtered every info-level warning out before rendering, so that whole branch of WarningsList was dead code and the existing SGI and above-the-cap notes were invisible too. Info warnings now render at the foot of the page, below the plan rather than above it.

Still to verifyThe 90-day figure is stated from knowledge, not from a checked FK page. Confirm it on the next annual pass.

RuleSFB 12:12

09Triplets and beyond probably split into the wrong tiers

multipleBirthExtraPerChild applies 90 sjukpenning + 90 lägstanivå uniformly to every child past the first. For the third and each further child the extra 180 days are believed to be entirely on sjukpenningnivå.

triplets app: 570 sjuk + 270 lägsta (total 840 ✓) law: 660 sjuk + 180 lägsta (total 840 ✓) 90 days misclassified — at 1 020 vs 180 kr/day, ≈ 75 600 kr

The total is right either way, which is why it survived the existing tests. Twins were correct.

Fixed in v0.29.0multipleBirthExtra now distinguishes the second child (90 + 90) from the third and every one after (180 income-based, 0 lägstanivå). Triplets come out 660 / 180. This is the one change resting on my reading of SFB 12:12 rather than a checked FK page, so it carries a TODO(confirm) for the annual pass.

11Annual figures are self-consistent but need their yearly re-check

Every derived amount reconciles against its own inputs — 592 000 × 0,97 × 0,8 / 365 = 1 258,6, capped to the stated 1 259 kr. The inputs themselves are the part that goes stale: prisbasbelopp 59 200, skiktgräns 643 000, average kommunalskatt 32,38 %. The file says June 2026; the next revision lands in January.

What checked out

Verified by running the modules, not by reading them. These needed no change, and the fixes above were built not to disturb them — the ordinary full-pace plan comes out byte-identical.

  • ✓Day budget. 480 = 390 + 90; 240 per parent; 90 reserved and non-transferable; twins +180 as 90 + 90.
  • ✓Benefit formula. SGI × 0,97 × 0,8 / 365, floored at grundnivå 250 and capped at 1 259 kr. The grundnivå floor takes over below 9 799 kr/mån, exactly where the formula crosses it.
  • ✓Two different ceilings. 10 prisbasbelopp for föräldrapenning, 7,5 for tillfällig — kept in separate modules so they cannot drift into each other.
  • ✓The birth-days overlap is lawful. The 10-dagar are tillfällig föräldrapenning, a different benefit, so both parents drawing at once is fine outside the dubbeldagar rules. The app merges them into one block for exactly the right reason.
  • ✓Dubbeldagar cannot come from reserved days, and are capped at ⌊transferable / 2⌋ and 60 — optimizer.ts:320–326 gets this right.
  • ✓240-day rule. First 180 income days drop to grundnivå, and days already used count toward the 180.
  • ✓Per-person taxation. 40 000 kr nets 30 716 as salary and 27 515 as benefit — a 3 201 kr gap that comes entirely from jobbskatteavdrag not applying to föräldrapenning. A flat household rate would hide this, and it is the single largest error such a model would make. Figures track the Skatteverket tables.
  • ✓Statlig inkomstskatt is applied to income after grundavdrag, matching how skiktgränsen is defined.
  • ✓Calendar arithmetic. Rounding drift in the date walk stays under 0,2 of a day across every pace tested — immaterial against figures already shown as approximations.
  • ✓Goal solving. The untilDate binary search converges on the fastest pace that still reaches the target, and the monotonicity it assumes holds.
  • ✓Day conservation. Across budget and manual modes the dated schedule draws 480,0–480,4 of 480 allocated days.
  • ✓The SGI floor after age 1 is applied at 5 days a week, and the lift is surfaced on the block where it happens rather than as a plan-level complaint.
  • ✓Reserved-day forfeiture is judged on the single-day split, correctly excluding the dubbeldagar added on top.
  • ✓Separation of concerns. The ruleset is a single pure data file with provenance dates and source links per constant. That is why this review could be done at all.

Standing caveat. This is a review of a planning aid against public sources, not legal advice. Amounts and edge rules change every January with the prisbasbelopp; the statute references above are pointers for verification, not a substitute for Försäkringskassan's own decision.

Two things to carry into the next annual pass. The triplets tier split (finding 09) and the 90-day application deadline (finding 08) are both implemented from my reading rather than from a checked Försäkringskassan page. Both are marked in the source. Everything else here was measured.
